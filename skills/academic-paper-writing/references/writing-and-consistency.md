# Writing and Consistency

Use this reference while drafting a passage, making a focused revision, or updating
results. Full-manuscript review is covered in
[Review and delivery](review-and-delivery.md).

## Describe the actual operation clearly

Understand the operation, its object, and the result or purpose needed to follow
the method. Use verbs that distinguish checking, removing, correcting, transforming,
and other materially different actions. When the implementation or intended meaning
is uncertain, identify the specific question rather than supplying a plausible
technical detail.

Write complete, direct sentences with clear subjects and relationships. Keep
parallel steps grammatically coordinated. Remove redundancy and ambiguity without
adding jargon or unnecessary qualifiers to make the prose sound scholarly.
Preserve already-clear wording and the collaborators' reasonable style.

Retain the scope, conditions, and strength of the claim when simplifying.
Do not introduce new results, causal explanations, novelty claims, or citations to
make a passage sound more complete. When a factual attribution needs verification,
use the actual source rather than inferring it from a familiar title or method name.

## Fit the explanation to its location

Let the main paper carry the essential problem, reasoning, method, and evidence.
Place detailed settings, prompts, extra derivations, and additional results in
supplementary material when that suits the manuscript and page budget. Give readers
a valid pointer when that detail is needed.

Provide a coherent passage for review when a paragraph is being rewritten. For
small edits, keep the surrounding structure intact. Adding supplementary detail
does not require expanding a main-paper sentence that is already accurate.

Use manuscript labels for figure and section references instead of hard-coded
numbers. Put explanations where readers need them, avoiding duplicate descriptions
across the body, caption, and supplementary text.

## Check consistency around the change

Check the locations affected by the meaning of the edit:

- Names, abbreviations, capitalization, symbols, indices, and units refer to the
  same concepts throughout the relevant passages.
- Definitions, conditions, method order, and descriptions agree between the main
  paper and supplement.
- Captions describe the current image, its panels, labels, and methods.
- Numerical statements and conclusions agree with the corresponding tables.
- Prompts, templates, or examples still match the protocol they illustrate when
  those materials are part of the requested change.

A related location may already be correct. Change it only when needed. If two
sources disagree on a scientific fact, explain the discrepancy and ask for the
missing decision rather than silently selecting one.

## Check numbers as part of filling them

Use the supplied values and existing evidence. Check relevant totals and components,
means and weights, units, display precision, repeated results, and statements of
improvement. Distinguish a percentage-point change from a relative percentage
change when interpreting quantitative claims.

Before applying a mathematical constraint, understand the metric's definition,
evaluation population, and aggregation order. Different averaging orders or
denominators can make apparently inconsistent results valid. Apply theoretical
checks only when their assumptions hold.

Compute derived values from available unrounded data when possible. If only rounded
table values are available, allow for their precision; do not change numbers solely
to make an equality hold at the displayed decimal places.

Reuse a result elsewhere only when it represents the same comparison and evaluation
condition. If a result is missing, retain an appropriate draft placeholder or flag
the gap; do not treat a missing value as zero or infer an experimental outcome.

Point out definite errors or suspicious relationships with a concise reason.
Passing consistency checks is not proof that the original experimental computation
was correct. Ask for raw data only when it is needed to settle a material issue,
not as a prerequisite for every table edit.

## Make result tables easy to read

Use native LaTeX tables by default for manuscript results. Preserve an existing
clear structure where possible. Make method names, conditions, metric names, units,
and comparison groups understandable without excessive abbreviations.

Keep the order and precision of comparable metrics consistent. Mark the best
results using the intended comparison set, metric direction, and treatment of ties.
Do not invent separate ranking groups or force a method into an inapplicable
condition merely to fill the table.

Prioritize legibility over packing every number into one table. Simplify headings,
group columns meaningfully, or move secondary detail to the supplement before
shrinking the type excessively. Align numerical entries and use spacing and rules
that clarify the comparisons.

After values or structure change, recheck emphasis and the associated prose.
Use the final PDF to confirm the table fits and remains readable.
